## Where A1 starts

A1 assumes the [script stage](/script/) is done: the learner reads unvocalised
Persian words slowly but correctly and writes the letters by hand. Short-vowel
marks stay on through roughly the first third of A1 — new vocabulary and model
sentences are vocalised, running text is not — and are gone by Unit 5. Latin
transliteration accompanies each new headword through the whole level and is
dropped at A2. Both fade-outs are deliberate and are set out in the
[course conventions](/appendices/style-guide/).

## The grammar spine

Persian repays a beginner faster than most languages: **no grammatical gender,
no case marking on ordinary nouns, no adjective agreement, and a verb system
whose present tense is one prefix plus one stem plus one of six endings.** The
budget that buys is spent on the two things that actually make Persian hard for
a German- or English-speaking beginner.

The first is the ***ezāfe***. An unstressed *-e* (or *-ye* after a vowel) links
a noun to whatever modifies it — {{< fa >}}کتابِ سارا{{< /fa >}} *ketāb-e Sārā*
"Sara's book", {{< fa >}}شهرِ بزرگ{{< /fa >}} *šahr-e bozorg* "the big city" —
and in ordinary writing it is not written at all. A learner who has not been
taught it explicitly will hear a syllable that is not on the page and conclude
that Persian is unpredictable. It is Unit 3, before it is needed everywhere
else.

The second is **register**. Written {{< fa >}}می‌روم{{< /fa >}} *mi-ravam* and
spoken {{< fa >}}می‌رم{{< /fa >}} *mi-ram* are the same word. A1 teaches the
written-formal form as the reference spine and names the colloquial one when it
appears in dialogue, without drilling it; the systematic treatment is A2.

## The recurring cast

A1 to B1 share a small ensemble anchored in Tehran: **Sārā**
({{< fa >}}سارا{{< /fa >}}), a university student, and **Kāve**
({{< fa >}}کاوه{{< /fa >}}), who runs a family bookshop near the bazaar. They
recur so that vocabulary compounds rather than resetting every unit, and so
that the *ezāfe* and the register distinction are met again and again in the
same mouths instead of being taught once and filed away.
