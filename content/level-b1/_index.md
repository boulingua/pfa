## What B1 adds

**The subjunctive.** Persian uses it far more than English does — after verbs of
wanting, being able, having to, and in nearly every subordinate clause that is
not a plain statement of fact. It is the single structure that separates a
learner who sounds like a phrasebook from one who sounds like a speaker, and it
gets three units of the twelve.

**Register as a working skill.** A2 taught learners to notice the difference
between written and spoken Persian. B1 asks them to produce in both: a formal
letter with its conventional openings and closings, and a spoken account of the
same content in colloquial Tehrani. The distance between the two versions is
the point of the exercise.

**The classical inheritance.** Persian's canonical poets — Hafez, Rumi, Saadi,
Ferdowsi — are centuries out of copyright and give a B1 reader something few
open courses can offer: real literature at a length a learner can actually
finish. B1 uses short couplets, always glossed, always cited to a verifiably
public-domain edition, as reading texts. Longer excerpts are reserved for the
levels this course does not claim.

## Exams

Exams after Units 3, 6 and 9, and a full B1 model exam at the end — reading,
listening, writing, speaking and mediation, with a marking key and grade
boundaries. The B1 model exam is the terminal assessment of the course as
declared.

## Where the course stops, and why

{{< callout type="warning" title="B2, C1 and C2 are declared gaps" >}}
This course declares conformance at **`core`** — A1 to B1. The levels above are
not thin here; they are absent, and are recorded as such. Openly-licensed,
CEFR-traceable Persian material at B2 and beyond is scarce, and building it
honestly is a multi-year undertaking rather than a matter of extending the
existing units with harder vocabulary. Declaring the gap is conformant with the
curriculum framework; quietly lowering the framework to cover it would not be.
{{< /callout >}}

## A note on the classical texts

Every couplet used in Units 9 and 10 carries its source: poet, work, and the
specific public-domain edition or transcription the text is taken from. The
poets are long out of copyright; a modern critical edition of them need not be,
and the difference matters. Where no verifiably public-domain text of a
particular line can be established, the line is not used.
