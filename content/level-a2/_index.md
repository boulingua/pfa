The level at which Persian stops being a set of phrases and becomes a language
a learner can narrate in: what happened, where they went, what hurts, what it
costs, and what they would rather have. It is also where the course confronts
the gap between the Persian that is written and the Persian that is spoken.
{{< /lead >}}
{{< /hero >}}

## What changes at A2

Three things.

**Transliteration stops.** From Unit 1 of this level, new vocabulary is given in
Persian script and glossed in English, without a Latin respelling. Learners who
completed A1 have read a few thousand running words by now; the crutch is
removed while the vocabulary is still concrete enough for the removal to be
survivable.

**The past tense arrives, and with it narration.** Persian's past is formed from
a second stem, and it is regular in a way German's is not. Once the simple past,
the imperfective past and the present perfect are in place, a learner can tell a
story — which is what most of A2's production tasks ask for.

**Colloquial forms are taught explicitly.** Not as an aside, but as a unit and
then as a running contrast: {{< fa >}}می‌روم{{< /fa >}} / {{< fa >}}می‌رم{{< /fa >}},
{{< fa >}}است{{< /fa >}} / {{< fa >}}ـه{{< /fa >}}, the colloquial plural and the
contracted object marker. Every dialogue from here on is labelled for register.
The written-formal form remains the reference spine — what learners produce in
writing and in exams — and the colloquial form is what they must understand and
may use in speech.

## The twelve units

| # | Unit | Focus | Skills |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Yesterday | The simple past; a first personal narrative | writing, speaking_production |
| 2 | Growing up | The imperfective past; habitual action in the past | reading, writing |
| 3 | Written and spoken | The register contrast, systematically | language_awareness, listening |
| 4 | At the bazaar | Bargaining, quantities, comparison | speaking_interaction, intercultural |
| 5 | Clothes and colours | Description; the comparative and superlative | speaking_production, writing |
| 6 | Travel and the ticket office | Booking, timetables, the Iranian calendar | reading, speaking_interaction |
| 7 | Health and the body | Symptoms, the pharmacy, giving advice | speaking_interaction, mediation |
| 8 | Work and study | Occupations, describing a working day | writing, speaking_production |
| 9 | Weather and seasons | Forecast language; Nowruz and the solar year | listening, intercultural |
| 10 | Invitations and *taʿārof* | Accepting, declining, the ritual of refusal | speaking_interaction, intercultural |
| 11 | The present perfect | Experience and result; "have you ever…" | language_awareness, writing |
| 12 | A short account | Putting the level's tenses into one written text | writing, mediation |

## Exams

Exams after Units 3, 6 and 9, and a full A2 model exam at the end of the level,
each with a committed PDF, a duration, a total, and a published grade boundary
table. From A2 the exams include a short **mediation** task — relaying the
content of a Persian notice or message in German or English, and the reverse —
because mediation is where a heritage-language learner's real competence
usually sits and where beginner courses usually have nothing.

{{< callout type="note" title="Prerequisite" >}}
A2 assumes [A1](/level-a1/) complete: unvocalised reading at sentence length,
the present tense, the *ezāfe*, numbers to a hundred, and roughly six hundred
words of active vocabulary.
