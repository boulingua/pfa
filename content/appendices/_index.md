## In preparation

Each of the following is authored alongside the level that first needs it, so
that its entries are drawn from units that exist rather than from a word list
assembled in advance.

- **Persian–English glossary** — every A1 headword with its transliteration and
  the unit that introduces it; A2 and B1 entries without transliteration, in
  line with the fade-out rule.
- **Pronunciation and script guide** — the six-vowel system, the consonants a
  German or English speaker mishears, the letters that share a sound
  ({{< fa >}}س ص ث{{< /fa >}}, {{< fa >}}ز ذ ض ظ{{< /fa >}},
  {{< fa >}}ت ط{{< /fa >}}, {{< fa >}}ق غ{{< /fa >}}), stress placement, and the
  full alphabet chart in all four positional forms.
- **Verb reference** — present and past stems for the irregular verbs a learner
  meets by B1, with the full paradigm of the present, simple past, imperfective
  past, present perfect and subjunctive, in written-formal and colloquial forms
  side by side.
- **Common errors** — *ezāfe* omitted or inserted where it does not belong,
  register mixing, the zero-width non-joiner left out, look-alike letters,
  Persian numerals set left to right, and the bidirectional-text traps that
  affect typing rather than speaking.
- **Numerals and the Iranian calendar** — the Solar Hijri year, month names, how
  dates are written, and the arithmetic of converting them.
- **Assessment rubrics** — the marking grids used by the exams: writing,
  speaking, and mediation, with descriptors per band.
- **A reader of classical couplets** — short public-domain extracts from Saadi,
  Hafez, Rumi and Ferdowsi, graded by level, each glossed and cited to the
  edition used.

{{< callout type="note" title="Why the appendices come last" >}}
A glossary written before the units is a vocabulary list with no provenance, and
a rubric written before the exams marks nothing in particular. Both are
generated from the units they document, which is why this section fills up level
by level rather than arriving complete.
{{< /callout >}}
