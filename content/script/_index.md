## Why a stage before A1

A Latin-script beginner course can teach the alphabet in the margins of Unit 1.
A Perso-Arabic course cannot. Thirty-two letters, each with up to four
contextual shapes, seven that refuse to join the letter after them, a set of
short vowels that ordinary writing simply omits, and a second set of digits:
that is a fortnight of teaching in its own right, and pretending otherwise is
how beginner courses end up permanently transliterated.

So the script comes first, as its own stage, with its own units, materials and
audio. Its can-do statements are mapped to pre-A1 descriptors where the
framework provides them and recorded as `no-official-descriptor` where it does
not — a declared gap rather than a silent one.

## What the stage covers

- The **thirty-two letters** of the Persian alphabet: name, sound, and the
  handwritten skeleton before the dots.
- The **four positional forms** — isolated, initial, medial, final — and why a
  letter changes shape without changing identity.
- The **seven non-connecting letters**, {{< fa >}}ا د ذ ر ز ژ و{{< /fa >}},
  which join to the right but never to the left, and the way they cut a word
  into visual blocks.
- The **four letters Persian adds** to the Arabic inventory —
  {{< fa >}}پ چ ژ گ{{< /fa >}} — together with the Persian
  {{< fa >}}ک{{< /fa >}} and {{< fa >}}ی{{< /fa >}}, which are not the Arabic
  letters they resemble.
- The **short vowels** {{< fa >}}ـَ ـِ ـُ{{< /fa >}} and the long vowels
  {{< fa >}}ا ی و{{< /fa >}}, first written in full, then withdrawn.
- The **Persian digits** {{< fa >}}۰۱۲۳۴۵۶۷۸۹{{< /fa >}}, which differ in shape
  from the Arabic-Eastern digits and are the ones used on Iranian signage.
- The **zero-width non-joiner** ({{< fa >}}نیم‌فاصله{{< /fa >}}), the invisible
  character that separates {{< fa >}}می‌روم{{< /fa >}} from a misspelling.

{{< callout type="note" title="How the script stage is assessed" >}}
There is no exam at the end of this stage. The gate is functional: a learner
who can read the eight sample word lists aloud, unvocalised, at reading speed
is ready for [Level A1](/level-a1/). The checklists at the end of each unit are
the instrument; the [pronunciation and script guide](/appendices/) collects
them in one place.
{{< /callout >}}

## A note on handwriting

The course teaches reading first and writing alongside it, in that order. The
printed *naskh* shapes on screen and the shapes a hand actually produces differ
enough — particularly for {{< fa >}}ک{{< /fa >}}, {{< fa >}}گ{{< /fa >}},
{{< fa >}}ه{{< /fa >}} and the final {{< fa >}}ی{{< /fa >}} — that the
worksheets give both: a *naskh* model to recognise and a cursive model to copy.
Iranian everyday handwriting leans towards *nastaʿlīq* proportions, and the
worksheets say so rather than pretending the printed form is what learners will
meet on a handwritten note.
